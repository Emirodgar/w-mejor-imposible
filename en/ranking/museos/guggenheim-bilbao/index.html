Guggenheim Bilbao

The Frank Gehry building that changed Bilbao: 1.3 million visitors in 2025, 69% of them from abroad.

Visitors in 2025: 1,305,003

No. 3 of 5 · Spain's 5 major art museums by visitors

Context

The Guggenheim Museum Bilbao is the work of the architect Frank Gehry. It was built between October 1993 and October 1997 on a former industrial quay at a bend in the Nervión estuary, on a site that symbolised the reclaiming of the riverbanks for the city.

The exterior is clad in some 33,000 thin sheets of titanium. Gehry worked with CATIA, a programme born in the aerospace industry, to translate his design into a structure. The building has 24,000 m², 9,000 of them for exhibitions, spread over three levels around an atrium.

Beside the entrance stands Puppy, Jeff Koons's 1992 sculpture covered in flowering plants, which the museum acquired in 1997. Since then its collection has focused on works from the mid-20th century to the present and complements those of the Solomon R. Guggenheim Foundation.
